B2C lead generation and organized systems for a coaching business

How I helped a massage therapist turned life coach attract, capture and nurture new clients online, and organize the systems behind her business. The client’s name is kept private.

B2C lead generationMailchimpLead magnetsBrand guidelinesCourse buildingCanva
The client

A massage therapist who became a life coach, teaching people about the nervous system, trauma responses and how to relax and respond to stress.

The offer

A high-ticket, 12-month coaching program with 24 live Zoom sessions, sold online to individuals.

The challenge

She was just starting out online. Her marketing and systems were scattered, and there was no clear path from stranger to client.

Why this is B2C lead generation

Individuals don’t buy a 12-month program from a stranger.

Her clients are people, not companies. They decide with their feelings as much as their logic, and a year-long, high-ticket program is a big personal commitment.

So the plan was to build trust step by step: give real value for free first, stay in touch through email, and only then invite people to the program.

The lead generation funnel

Four stages, from first seeing her post to joining the program, and what I built at each one.

1Attract
Get her in front of the right people
Drafted and scheduled social media posts on her topics
Helped promote her book to reach new readers
Kept every post on-brand with the brand guidelines
2Capture
Turn interest into an email address
Created free guides and worksheets as lead magnets
Gave each one a clear sign-up path
Connected sign-ups to her Mailchimp list
3Nurture
Build trust before asking for a big commitment
Built Mailchimp workflows that welcome new leads
Followed up automatically with helpful content
Kept the tone warm and personal, like her coaching
4Convert
Invite ready leads to the 12-month program
Pointed emails and posts to her program
Supported the new website, her home base, with her web designer
Made the course ready so new clients could start right away

Organizing the systems

A funnel only works if what’s behind it is organized. Here’s what changed.

Before
After
Brand
Every post, guide and email looked a little different
Brand guidelines with her colors, fonts, tone of voice and how to use them
Email
New leads had no automatic follow-up
Mailchimp workflows that welcome and nurture every new lead
Course
Program content wasn’t in one organized place
An online course on a coaching platform, with lessons and quizzes in order
Content
Posts were created whenever there was time
Drafted posts ready in advance, plus reusable worksheets and guides
Website
No central home for her online business
A new website, built with her web designer, that ties everything together
The outcome
A clear path from first post to program sign-up
One consistent brand across social, email and web
Automatic follow-up for every new lead
A ready-to-sell course, so she could focus on coaching
What I took away
High-ticket B2C offers need trust before a sales pitch
Lead magnets work best when they solve one small problem
Organized systems are what make marketing repeatable
